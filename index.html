Help & shortcuts

Placing an object

Drag and drop
This is THE gesture: grab a thumbnail on the left and drop it where you want it. The preview shows where the object lands; on a wall-mounted object the target wall is highlighted, and the preview fades when no wall is within reach.
Click the thumbnail
Arms the drop (the thumbnail lights up): the next click on the plan places the object under the pointer. Enter puts it at the centre of the view, Esc cancels.
By finger
Tap the thumbnail (the drawer closes), then tap the spot on the plan: the object lands under your finger.

Copy & paste

Ctrl+C
Copies the selection, one object or many, keeping their layout.
Ctrl+X
Cuts the selection: it leaves the plan and waits in the clipboard.
Ctrl+V
Pastes under the cursor (otherwise offset from the original). One paste = one Ctrl+Z. A copy is always born unlocked, and a wall-mounted object reattaches to the wall nearest the paste point.
Another tab
The copy also goes to the system clipboard: Ctrl+V in another tab of the plan finds it again.

Moving & selecting

Drag
Grab a piece of furniture and move it. Chairs tucked under a table follow it.
Marquee
Drag on empty space to select several pieces (rubber band).
Ctrl+click
Adds or removes a piece from the selection.
Group
Drag a selected piece to move the whole group. Del deletes them all.
Long press
Touch: hold your finger on a piece to add it to the selection.
Alt+drag
Duplicates the piece and moves the copy.
Double-click
Rotates the piece by 90°. On a door, flips the hinge side.
Double-click the name
Renames instead of rotating, on the label itself: the box opens where the name is, with the text selected, so typing replaces it. Enter keeps it, Esc throws it away. A piece still carrying its catalogue name paints no label, so there is nothing to double-click until you have named it.
Shift+drag
Fine mode. The object follows your hand five times slower, so the exact centimetre becomes reachable at working zoom. The 5 cm grid is switched off while you hold it. Works for moving and resizing furniture, and for sliding a door or window along its wall.
Even spacing
Select three or more objects lined up in one direction: the inspector shows the measured gaps and a button that makes them equal. The two end objects stay put, so on a bed between two bedside tables it is the bed that centres itself. With only two objects there is a single gap and nothing to even out. To set an exact gap instead, type it in the small box and press Enter: that keeps the first object where it is and pushes the rest along, because the block has to change size for the gap to be the one you asked for.

Resizing

Handles
Pull the handles of a selected piece; the opposite side stays put.
By keyboard
While dragging, type the dimension in cm then Enter. Tab switches to the other side (corner), Esc cancels.
Inspector
Set width and depth to the exact figure in the right-hand panel.

Walls

Walls mode
The Walls button, or double-click on the plan. Furniture is locked.
Move a wall
Pull a partition: the two rooms it separates adjust together.
Draw a wall
The Draw a wall button, then drag: the wall runs across and cuts the room in two.
Freehand
The Freehand button, then draw a path with the mouse: it becomes a chain of straight walls in one go, corners included, instead of two lines that have to meet exactly. The path's own two ends stay where you drew them, like a free partition; every corner in between is a true junction, shared by the two walls it joins. Alt keeps the path as drawn, with no forced right angle.
Alt+drag a wall
Draws a free partition: no right angle forced, no magnet, and above all both ends stay where you put them. A normal wall pushes each end until it meets something, and failing that all the way to the facade, which is why walls usually attach to something.
Length
On a selected wall, type the exact length in centimetres. It stretches the free end only: the other one is usually a junction, and moving it would break the room on the far side. A facade works too, by sliding the next facade along, so the corners stay square. Setting a length also makes a partition Free, otherwise it would immediately stretch back out.
Neighbour lengths
While you drag a wall, every wall touching it is measured too: you move a wall to size the room next to it, and that room is bounded by its neighbours.
Through / Free
On a selected partition, switches between the two: Through extends both ends until they meet something, Free leaves them alone. A free wall is still trimmed at the facade: free does not mean allowed to leave the flat.
Delete a wall
Select it and click the cross: the two rooms merge.
Facades
Pull an edge or a corner of the outline; Shift forces the horizontal/vertical axis.
Rooms
A room is not drawn: it is COMPUTED from the walls. Click its name to open its card, or double-click the name to rename it straight away.

Doors & wall fittings

Leaf
The arc shows the swing. Double-click (or Rotate 90°) flips the hinge side.
Swing direction
The Swing direction button: opens inwards or outwards.
From the corner
In the inspector, place the door at an exact distance from the corner of the wall.
Windows, sockets, wall lights
They stick to the nearest wall and slide along it; the arrow keys nudge them.
How a window opens
On a selected window: None (fixed, or top-hung — it opens vertically, so nothing is drawn on the floor), Single (one casement, with Hinge side and Swing direction like a door), or Double (two casements meeting in the middle). Windows start as None, so nothing on your plan changes until you set one.
Several at once
Select several openings on the same wall and drag one: they all move together, keeping their spacing. If one hits a limit the whole train stops, so the gaps are never crushed.
Wall face
A wall light, a socket or an RJ45 lands on the face the cursor is on: drop it from the hallway and it faces the hallway. Drag it through its wall to move it to the other face, or use Flip side (R key).
Back to back
Two sockets (or two wall lights) can occupy the SAME spot on BOTH faces of a wall: those are two different surfaces. On the same face, they push each other apart. A door or a window, on the other hand, goes through the wall: it is in the way on both sides.

Projector

Throw ratio
The number on the spec sheet: image width = distance ÷ ratio. Enter it and the beam appears on the plan. 1.5 is typical; an ultra-short-throw is around 0.25, which puts a 120 cm image 30 cm from the wall.
Aimed at
Pair the projector with a projection screen. It then shows the real distance, the image width you will get, and whether the image is wider or narrower than the screen.
Min. distance
The closest the projector can still focus, from its spec sheet. Below it the beam turns red. Leave it empty and nothing is claimed: a short-throw sitting close to the wall is correct, not a mistake.

Measuring

Tape measure
The 📏 Measure button, then click two points; the distance appears.
Probe cursor
The cursor shows the distances to the surrounding walls live.
Exact dimension
Points snap to walls, corners and furniture so the measurement is true.

Display

Zoom
The wheel (or a two-finger pinch) zooms around the cursor.
Pan the view
Right-click drag, or Space+drag, or two fingers.
Fit
The Fit button: reframes on the whole flat.
Layers
The Furniture / Lighting / Sockets boxes show or hide each family.
By room / By kind
Above the furniture list: group it by room (Bedroom, Kitchen…) or by kind (Seating, Storage, Lighting…). Your own setting; it does not follow the plan.
Plans
File → Plans… lists your plans, creates one, and renames one by double-clicking its name. A new plan starts empty, and switching reloads the page.
Circulation
Show circulation shades the walkable floor; Circulation scores the layout. It also flags two objects left sitting in the same place — except a tumble dryer stacked on a washing machine, a rug under furniture, or anything mounted at height.

Together

Cursors
The other person's cursor and selection show up live.
Chat
The 💬 button at the bottom right, to talk inside the plan.
Sync
Changes are shared automatically; the status chip in the bar says so.
